September 2026 was Helsinki's wettest since 2012. Kaisaniemi, the weather station in central Helsinki whose record goes back to 1844, collected <V mm={89.8} />, <Mark>161% of normal</Mark>, and almost half of it came in the last ten days. At least <V mm={1} d={0} /> fell on 16 days, almost twice the usual 8.6; since 1961 only 1974 and 2012 had more. The sun still logged about as many hours as in an ordinary September, though it came in all-or-nothing days.

Temperatures were mild. The month averaged <V t={13.7} />, <V dd={1.4} /> above the official 1991–2020 normal and <V dd={1.1} /> above the average of the last 30 Septembers. With wind and humidity factored in it felt like <V t={11.4} />, also more than a degree warmer than normal. It was the seventh-warmest September since 1961, and yet <Mark>the coolest of the last four</Mark>: 2023, 2024, and 2025 were all warmer.

<SectionHead id="two-yardsticks" title="Two yardsticks" art="rulers" report={data} />

A climate normal is a 30-year average. The current official period, set by the World Meteorological Organization, is 1991–2020, and it is what the Finnish Meteorological Institute (FMI) publishes for every station. For September at Kaisaniemi that means <V t={12.3} /> and <V mm={55.7} /> of rain. It stays the reference until 2001–2030 replaces it in the early 2030s.

A fixed period ages, and in a warming climate it ages in one direction. So this report also uses a running normal: the 30 most recent complete Septembers, 1996–2025.

For September in Helsinki the two yardsticks are about a third of a degree apart: <V t={12.3} /> against <V t={12.65} d={2} />. The gap comes from a five-year swap: 1991–1995, which averaged <V t={11.1} />, left the window, and 2021–2025, which averaged <V t={13.4} />, joined it. Over a longer view the shift is larger. The 1961–1990 normal was <V t={11.0} />, and the running 30-year mean bottomed out at <V t={10.8} /> for 1968–1997 before climbing to today's <V t={12.65} d={2} />.

<ReportChart title="Every September at Kaisaniemi since 1961" spec={septembersChart(data.septembers, data.standard_normals, 2026)} />

Against either yardstick, 2026 was clearly above normal but not extreme. Three Septembers in 1991–2020 were warmer (2006, 2018, and 2020), and six in 1996–2025. Measured against the 1961–1990 climate, though, it would have been the warmest September of all 30.

The rain normal has barely moved: <V mm={55.7} /> for 1991–2020 and <V mm={56.0} /> for 1996–2025. Both sit well below the <V mm={74.1} /> of 1961–1990, when Helsinki's Septembers were wetter.

<SectionHead id="sunshine" title="Sun in all-or-nothing days" art="recorder" report={data} />

For all its rain, September 2026 got its usual share of sun. Kaisaniemi has no sunshine recorder, so this section uses Helsinki-Vantaa airport, which also has an official normal, and Kumpula, three kilometers away. Helsinki-Vantaa logged <Mark>154.8 hours of sunshine</Mark>, 104% of the 1991–2020 normal of 149.5 hours and almost exactly its average since 1999 (153.8 hours). That puts it 13th of the 28 Septembers on record there, right in the middle. Kumpula, in the city, saw 159.5 hours against its average of 147.7 since 2006.

<SectionHead id="heating-and-thermal-autumn" title="Heating and thermal autumn" art="radiator" report={data} />

September 2026 needed almost no heating. Heating degree days, FMI's measure of heating demand, only start to add up once a day's mean temperature drops below <V t={10} d={0} />. That happened just once, on the 23rd, so Kaisaniemi logged <Mark>7 degree days, where a normal September has 63</Mark>. Only two Septembers since 1961 needed less: 1992 and 2023, which needed none.

The same <V t={10} d={0} /> line marks thermal autumn, which begins once the daily mean stays below it for good. Apart from the 23rd, September stayed above it, so thermal autumn did not begin during the month.

<SectionHead id="clouds" title="Clouds brought the rain and the mildness" report={data} />

Southwesterly winds and passing fronts wrapped the city in cloud, and the cloud kept the nights from cooling, so the gray days were both the wet ones and the mild ones. Between the fronts, high pressure cleared the sky and brought the month's usual share of sun. The result was a September with no real heat and no real cold, and the wettest Helsinki has seen since 2012.

<SectionHead id="about-the-data" title="About the data" art="stamp" report={data} />

- **Stations:** Helsinki Kaisaniemi (FMISID 100971); Kumpula, Harmaja, and Helsinki-Vantaa for the comparison table. Kumpula has too short a record for normals of its own, Harmaja has no rain gauge, and Kumpula's gauge missed an hour of the month.
- **September 2026:** The 10-minute observations this site collects from FMI's open data, grouped into Helsinki calendar days: daily mean, highest and lowest temperature, and hourly rainfall totals. The month has no gaps.
- **History:** FMI's daily observations for 1961–2025, and its hourly observations for wind, gusts, and humidity in 1996–2025. Kaisaniemi's record goes back to 1844, but the rankings here start in 1961.
- **Normals:** The 1991–2020 temperature and rainfall normals are FMI's official values. The running 1996–2025 normal and the day counts (rainy days, days reaching <V t={20} d={0} />, nights below <V t={5} d={0} />) are computed from the same history. Daily normal curves average each calendar day with the week on either side (five days for rain), as on this site's climate normals card.
- **Feels-like:** Hourly mean temperature, wind, and humidity through the site's feels-like formula (wind chill, plus a humidity correction above <V t={14.5} />; no solar radiation), the same way the feels-like normals are built. FMI's hourly record at Kaisaniemi starts in 1996, so the 1991–2020 feels-like normal covers 1996–2020 and the feels-like rankings start in 1996.
- **Sunshine:** FMI's minute-by-minute sunshine and global radiation observations at Helsinki-Vantaa and Kumpula, whose September records start in 1999 and 2006, summed over Helsinki calendar days. The 1991–2020 sunshine and radiation normals are FMI's official values for Helsinki-Vantaa. Daylight is the time the sun is above the horizon.
- **Daytime:** The hours from 8 am to 9 pm Helsinki time, as in WeatherSpark's tourism score. Temperature and feels-like are compared with the same hours in FMI's hourly record from 1996. A rainy hour is one in which the gauge measured any rain; FMI's hourly rain totals for Kaisaniemi start in 2013.
- **Heating:** FMI's heating degree days. In autumn, each day with a mean temperature below <V t={10} d={0} /> adds the gap between that mean and <V t={17} d={0} />.
- **Rounding:** FMI's own monthly statistics can differ from these figures by a tenth of a degree or a millimeter or two.
