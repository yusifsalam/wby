<SectionHead id="two-yardsticks" title="Two yardsticks" art="rulers" report={data} />

A climate normal is a thirty-year average. The current official period, set by the World Meteorological Organization, is 1991–2020, and it is what the Finnish Meteorological Institute (FMI) publishes for every station. For September at Kaisaniemi that means <V t={12.3} /> and <V mm={55.7} /> of rain. It stays the reference until 2001–2030 replaces it in the early 2030s.

A fixed period ages, and in a warming climate it ages in one direction. So this report also uses a running normal: the thirty most recent complete Septembers, 1996–2025.

For September in Helsinki the two yardsticks are about a third of a degree apart: <V t={12.3} /> against <V t={12.65} d={2} />. The gap is the five Septembers that left the window (1991–1995, averaging <V t={11.1} />) being replaced by five much warmer ones (2021–2025, averaging <V t={13.4} />). Over a longer view the shift is larger. The 1961–1990 normal was <V t={11.0} />, and the running thirty-year mean bottomed out at <V t={10.8} /> for 1968–1997 before climbing to today's <V t={12.65} d={2} />.

<ReportChart title="Every September at Kaisaniemi since 1961" spec={septembersChart(data.septembers, data.standard_normals, 2026)} />

Against either yardstick, 2026 was clearly warm but not extreme. Three Septembers in 1991–2020 were warmer (2006, 2018 and 2020), and six in 1996–2025. Measured against the 1961–1990 climate, though, it would have been the warmest September of all thirty.

The rain normal has barely moved: <V mm={55.7} /> for 1991–2020 and <V mm={56.0} /> for 1996–2025. Both sit well below the <V mm={74.1} /> of 1961–1990, when Helsinki's Septembers were wetter.

Worked out hour by hour, September 2026 felt like <V t={11.4} /> on average: <V dd={2.3} /> cooler than the thermometer, but <V dd={1.3} /> warmer than the 1991–2020 normal (<V t={10.05} d={2} />) and <V dd={1.2} /> warmer than 1996–2025 (<V t={10.23} d={2} />).

<SectionHead id="sunshine" title="Sunshine" art="recorder" report={data} />

Kaisaniemi doesn't record sunshine. Helsinki's sunshine recorders are at Kumpula, three kilometres away, and at Helsinki-Vantaa airport, which also has an official normal. For all its rain, September 2026 got its usual share. Helsinki-Vantaa logged <Mark>154.8 hours of sunshine</Mark>, 104% of the 1991–2020 normal of 149.5 hours and almost exactly its average since 1999 (153.8 hours). That puts it 13th of the 28 Septembers on record there, right in the middle. Kumpula, in the city, saw 159.5 hours against its average of 147.7 since 2006.

What was unusual was how the sunshine came: in all-or-nothing days. Nine days had less than half an hour of sun, against five in an average September, and ten had eight hours or more, against eight. The rain fell on the grey days, as fronts clouded the sky over completely, while the high pressure in between brought clear skies. The 14th was the sunniest day, with 11.8 hours out of a possible 13.1, and the 22nd, 27th and 30th came close.

The grey days were also the mild ones. The nine dullest days averaged <V dt={1.8} /> against the running normal, with nights <V dd={3.3} /> warmer than usual under the cloud, while the ten sunniest averaged only <V dt={0.2} />: warm afternoons, but clear nights <V dd={0.8} /> cooler than normal. Over the month the sun shone for 40% of the hours it was up, and each square metre of ground received 274 MJ of solar energy, 101% of normal.

<SectionHead id="out-and-about" title="Out and about" report={data} />

A monthly mean gives the small hours as much weight as the afternoon, though few people are outdoors at 4 am. That is 13 hours a day, 390 over the month.

<SectionHead id="about-the-data" title="About the data" art="stamp" report={data} />

- **Station.** Helsinki Kaisaniemi (FMISID 100971); Kumpula, Harmaja and Helsinki-Vantaa for the comparison table.
- **September 2026.** The 10-minute observations this site collects from FMI's open data, grouped into Helsinki calendar days: daily mean, highest and lowest temperature, and hourly rainfall totals. The month has no gaps.
- **History.** FMI's daily observations for 1961–2025, and its hourly observations for wind, gusts and humidity in 1996–2025. Kaisaniemi's record goes back to 1844, but the rankings here start in 1961.
- **Normals.** The 1991–2020 temperature and rainfall normals are FMI's official values. The running 1996–2025 normal and the day counts (rainy days, warm days, cold nights) are computed from the same history. Daily normal curves average each calendar day with the week either side (five days for rain), as on this site's climate normals card.
- **Feels-like.** Hourly mean temperature, wind and humidity through the site's feels-like formula (wind chill, plus a humidity correction above <V t={14.5} />; no solar radiation), the same way the feels-like normals are built. FMI's hourly record at Kaisaniemi starts in 1996, so the 1991–2020 feels-like normal covers 1996–2020 and the feels-like rankings start in 1996.
- **Sunshine.** FMI's minute-by-minute sunshine and global radiation observations at Helsinki-Vantaa (from 1999) and Kumpula (from 2005), summed over Helsinki calendar days. The 1991–2020 sunshine and radiation normals are FMI's official values for Helsinki-Vantaa. Daylight is the time the sun is above the horizon.
- **Daytime.** The hours from 8 am to 9 pm Helsinki time, as in WeatherSpark's tourism score. Temperature and feels-like are compared with the same hours in FMI's hourly record from 1996. A rainy hour is one in which the gauge measured any rain; FMI's hourly rain totals for Kaisaniemi start in 2013.
- FMI's own monthly statistics can differ from these figures by a tenth of a degree or a millimetre or two.
