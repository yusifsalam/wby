<SectionHead id="no-frost" title="No frost, and a late thermal autumn" art="frost" report={data} />

Kaisaniemi did not drop below freezing in October. The lowest measured temperature was <V t={1.1} />, at 6:20 am on the 19th. In a typical year (1991–2020), the city center's first frost comes around October 15, and <Mark>only two of those 30 years had none by the end of October</Mark>: 2008 and 2011. The suburbs had theirs. Helsinki-Vantaa airport first went below zero on the 18th and Kumpula on the 19th.

Thermal autumn begins once the daily mean stays below <V t={10} d={0} /> for good. The 28th averaged <V t={10.1} />, the last day of the month to reach it, so thermal autumn began on the 29th, provided November stays below <V t={10} d={0} />. That's late for the city center. By this definition, the median start was October 19 in 1991–2020 and October 23 in 1996–2025. Four of the 30 years in 1991–2020 waited until November, and so did three of the last six: 2020, 2022, and 2025.

The mild days kept heating demand low. Heating degree days only start to add up once a day's mean drops below <V t={10} d={0} />, and in October Kaisaniemi logged <Mark>117 degree days, where a normal October has 286</Mark>. Only 1961 needed less.

<SectionHead id="southerly-and-gray" title="Southerly winds and cloud kept it mild" report={data} />

Most of October's weather came from the south. The wind blew from between southeast and southwest 54% of the time, and 20 of the 31 days were overcast. The southerly air kept the days mild and the cloud kept the nights from cooling, so highs and lows both ran about <V dd={3} d={0} /> above the 1991–2020 normal. <SectionHead id="about-the-data" title="About the data" art="stamp" report={data} />

- **Stations:** Helsinki Kaisaniemi (FMISID 100971); Kumpula, Harmaja, and Helsinki-Vantaa for the comparison table. Kumpula has too short a record for normals of its own, and Harmaja has no rain gauge. The airport's rain record is missing one hour on the 30th; its rain-intensity sensor read zero throughout, so that hour counts as dry.
- **October 2026:** The 10-minute observations this site collects from FMI's open data, grouped into Helsinki calendar days. From about 10:30 am on October 19 until the morning of the 23rd, Kaisaniemi reported only wind, along with a temperature every three hours. That stretch is filled from Kumpula, three kilometers away, adjusted by the usual difference between the two stations at each hour of the day and passed through every Kaisaniemi reading there is. Rain for those hours is Kumpula's. The filled daily means are within <V dd={0.3} /> of FMI's own daily means for those days. The night of the 20th, which the fill puts at about <V t={0.8} />, is the coldest in the chart. The lowest temperature actually measured is <V t={1.1} />, and FMI's own minimum for the 20th, published since, is <V t={0.5} />.
- **History:** FMI's daily observations for 1961–2025, and its hourly observations for wind, gusts, and humidity in 1996–2025. The 2026 daily highs and lows come from the 10-minute observations, the history from FMI's daily maximums and minimums. FMI's own October 2026 maximums average <V t={12.1} />, so the record for daytime highs holds under either method.
- **Normals:** The 1991–2020 and running 1996–2025 normals are the means of October over each period, computed from the same history. Daily normal curves average each calendar day with the week on either side (five days for rain), as on this site's climate normals card.
- **Frost and thermal autumn:** A frost is a daily low below <V t={0} d={0} /> at two meters. Thermal autumn begins the day after the last day of the year with a mean of at least <V t={10} d={0} />.
- **Feels-like:** Hourly mean temperature, wind, and humidity through the site's feels-like formula (wind chill, plus a humidity correction above <V t={14.5} />; no solar radiation). FMI's hourly record at Kaisaniemi starts in 1996.
- **Sunshine:** FMI's minute-by-minute sunshine and global radiation observations at Helsinki-Vantaa and Kumpula, whose October records start in 1999 and 2006.
- **Daytime:** The hours from 8 am to 9 pm Helsinki time. A rainy hour is one in which the gauge measured any rain; FMI's hourly rain totals for Kaisaniemi start in 2013. Sunset times are computed for central Helsinki. Weekend comparisons use Helsinki-Vantaa's sunshine since 1999 and Kaisaniemi's hourly rain since 2013, per weekend day, since an October has eight to ten of them.
- **Heating:** FMI's heating degree days. In autumn, each day with a mean temperature below <V t={10} d={0} /> adds the gap between that mean and <V t={17} d={0} />.
